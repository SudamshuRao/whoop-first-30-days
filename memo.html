Memo: what to do when a member lapses

A recommendation and test plan for WHOOP, from public data

TipRecommendation

Test a single, specific check-in on day 14 of a lapse, and judge it by whether members come back, week by week. Expect a small effect that fades: plan the test to detect a 2-point lift, which needs about 19,316 lapsed members.

Why lapses are the right moment

  • They are common. In 10,321 lapses (14+ days without a workout) among long-term Endomondo members, the median member lapsed about 2.0 times a year.
  • Most are temporary, so there is something to win. 55% were back within 14 days of the lapse being detected (95% CI 54% to 56%); the median lapse lasted 26 days.
  • The first 30 days after a comeback are fragile. When a member returning from 12+ weeks away goes quiet again, only 36% are back within 14 days. These members are the ones a check-in could help most.
  • Autumn lapses ran longer than winter ones (51% vs 61% back within 14 days), so the test should run across seasons or randomize within them.

What a nudge can and cannot do

In the HeartSteps trial, a suggestion raised steps in the next 30 minutes by +16.1% (95% CI +0.5% to +34.2%). Two details shape the design:

  • It fades. The effect was +55% on day 0 and +4% by day 30, and ratings did not warn that it was happening. So measure the effect by week, not only overall.
  • Specific beats generic. Walking suggestions worked (+23%); generic “get up and move” prompts did not (+7%, CI includes zero). The check-in should name one concrete action.

What members say (indicative only)

Of 1,413 Google Play reviews since the WHOOP 5.0 launch, 41% are negative and 50% positive; 39% are general praise or complaint with no specific topic. The words most over-represented in negative reviews include membership, cancel, money, paid and trial, alongside crashes and stopped working; in positive ones, helps, habits, goals and accountable (analysis/review_explore.py). Theme shares are not claimed: checked blind against hand labels, only the data-accuracy keyword rule held up (6 of 6 tags confirmed). Reviews are also self-selected and far more negative than WHOOP’s store rating.

The test

Who Members reaching day 14 without a workout, each enrolled once
Arms Specific check-in vs no message, 50/50
Primary metric Back within 14 days (a recorded workout)
Also report The effect by week since enrolment; back within 28 days
Guardrails Notification opt-outs; membership cancellations within 30 days
Lift to detect Members needed Weeks to enrol, 50k eligible Weeks, 100k eligible
+1 point 77,454 37 19
+2 points 19,316 9 5
+3 points 8,562 4 2
+5 points 3,064 1 1

80% power, two-sided α = 0.05, baseline 55%, from 2.17 lapses per member-year; add 2 weeks for the outcome window. At a 50,000-member eligible base, a 2-point lift takes about 9 weeks. A simulation on the real lapse outcomes confirmed the sizing (79.5% power). Each member is enrolled once because a member’s lapses resemble each other: counting every lapse would understate uncertainty by a factor of 1.40. Adjusting for what is known at detection explains only 4.9% of the outcome, so it would shrink the test only slightly.

What this cannot tell us

  • None of this is WHOOP data. Endomondo members (2007-2015) were long-term, self-selected trackers; HeartSteps had 37 sedentary adults receiving walking prompts. WHOOP’s baseline and effect sizes will differ, which is why the plan sizes for a range of lifts.
  • Lapse findings are associations, not causes. Only a randomized test answers whether a check-in brings members back.
  • The review themes are not validated beyond the accuracy rule; they describe what members talk about, not how many feel it.
 

Public data only: HeartSteps V1 (Klasnja et al., 2019; CC BY 4.0), FitRec/Endomondo (Ni et al., 2019) and public Google Play reviews of the WHOOP app. No WHOOP internal data.
