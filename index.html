First 30 Days

What should WHOOP do when a member stops training? An end-to-end product analysis on public data.

WHOOP is adding proactive features such as Coach check-ins. This project asks where a proactive message could matter most, and how WHOOP should test it: when a member stops training. It uses three public datasets, a dbt and DuckDB pipeline, and a pre-registered experiment readout, and ends in a one-page recommendation.

1. Lapses are common and recoverable

The median member lapsed (14+ days without a workout) about 2.0 times a year, and 55% were back within 14 days. Members returning from 12+ weeks away were the most fragile: only 36% came back.

Lapses & returns →

2. Nudges work a little, then fade

In a micro-randomized trial, a suggestion raised 30-minute steps by +16.1% (95% CI +0.5% to +34.2%), from +55% on day 0 to +4% by day 30. Specific walking prompts worked (+23%); generic ones did not.

Experiment readout →

3. The test is sized

To detect a 2-point lift in members coming back, WHOOP would need about 19,316 lapsed members: roughly 9 weeks of enrolment from a 50,000-member eligible base.

TipRecommendation

Test one specific check-in on day 14 of a lapse, randomized per member, and judge it by whether members come back, week by week, with opt-outs and cancellations as guardrails. Read the memo.

How it is built

  • Every analysis page reads only the marts, and every number on them is computed at build time; none is typed in by hand. (The data audit deliberately reads the raw files.)
  • The experiment plan was committed before any outcome was analyzed (see the readout’s commit history), and the readout replicates the published result.
  • Leakage was found and fixed. Comparing members with their whole history, future included, overstated the “slowdown before a lapse” signal; a past-only baseline replaced it (details).
  • Not everything worked. Keyword themes for app reviews were checked blind against hand labels, and only one rule held up, so the memo claims no theme shares. The data audit explains what each dataset can and cannot support.
